How to Configure Grant Controls in Microsoft Entra

17 November 2026New feature+31 / −1 lines

In brief

The documentation adds guidance for requiring identity verification, including eligible profiles, resource and onboarding/recovery scopes, licensing, limitations, and the distinction from credential provenance and risk remediation.

What Entra admins need to know

Administrators can use the guidance to configure appropriate verification policies, preserve emergency access, and avoid relying on report-only evaluation for onboarding or recovery enforcement.

Conditional Access: Grant

  • Require approved client app
  • Require app protection policy
  • Require password change
  • Require identity verification

When admins choose to combine these options, they can use the following methods:

If your organization created terms of use, other options might be visible under grant controls. These options allow admins to require acknowledgment of terms of use as a condition of accessing the resources that the policy protects. You can find more information about terms of use in Microsoft Entra terms of use.

Require identity verification

The identity verification grant control requires users to verify their identity for protected access. Configure an eligible verification profile and apply the requirement to the supported resource or action scope in your tenant.

Identity verification and credential provenance are distinct. An identity verification requirement doesn't, by itself, establish the provenance of the specific credential used at sign-in. During preview, credential provenance is recorded only for passkey credentials.

Check Identity verification licensing for the requirements that apply to your scenario. License requirements for identity verification are separate from the existing generic authentication-strength guidance.

Configure an eligible identity verification profile, then follow the guide for your scenario:

  • Require identity verification for resource access for resource policy design, supported issuance testing, and failure handling.
  • Protect onboarding and recovery for bootstrap policy boundaries.
  • Configure risk policies for the separate risk scenario.

Use onboarding and recovery user-action targeting to protect bootstrap separately from ordinary resource access. Confirm the target and exclusion options available in your tenant. Preserve emergency access, and keep exclusions scoped and approved rather than removing ordinary resource protection.

!Note

Terms of use and Require authentication strength aren't supported for the onboarding and recovery user action. Most other Microsoft Entra-specific controls also aren't supported for this action. Review the onboarding and recovery control limitations before combining controls. This limitation doesn't apply to ordinary resource-access policies.

Require risk remediation remains a distinct control with the behavior described above. The identity verification requirement isn't a rename or an automatic change to that control.

Report-only evaluation isn't enforcement and doesn't prove that verification completed. Report-only guidance excludes user-action policies from evaluation; don't rely on it for onboarding and recovery without confirming support for that action.

Multiple grant controls

When multiple grant controls apply to a user, understand that Conditional Access policies follow a specific validation order by design. For example, if a user has two policies requiring multifactor authentication (MFA) and Terms of Use (ToU), Conditional Access validates the user's MFA claim first, then the ToU.
